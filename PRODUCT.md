## Brand

Immigration Evaluation Center (`immigrationevaluation.center`) is the national parent brand for state and city service pages, including the existing Immigration Evaluations of Texas practice.

## Users

Immigrants, qualifying relatives, and families who need a psychological evaluation for an immigration matter, as well as immigration attorneys seeking a responsive licensed clinician and a thorough, case-relevant report. Visitors may be coping with trauma, abuse, persecution, trafficking, separation, or uncertainty and need language that feels safe, clear, and respectful.

## Product Purpose

Help visitors understand immigration psychological evaluations, identify whether the service may fit their case, find an appropriately licensed clinician in their state, and request a confidential consultation. The service supports hardship waivers, asylum, U-Visas, VAWA, T-Visas, SIJS, cancellation of removal, J-Visa matters, Adam Walsh matters, removal of conditions, and other case-specific needs.

## Brand Personality

Compassionate, clinically rigorous, calm, culturally responsive, and dignified. The voice should acknowledge distress without amplifying fear. Explain the process plainly, avoid legal jargon where possible, and never promise approval or a favorable case outcome.

## Copy Principles

- Put the person before the paperwork.
- Explain how clinical evidence can document trauma or hardship without claiming it guarantees success.
- Distinguish psychological evaluation from legal representation and therapy.
- Make confidentiality, telehealth, timing, and attorney coordination easy to understand.
- Use the parent brand on national pages and localize clinician licensure, service areas, and contact details on child pages.

## Anti-references

Avoid visa-eligibility quizzes, pathway scoring, approval percentages, fabricated testimonials, legal advice, outcome guarantees, fear-based urgency, and generic lead-generation language. Do not imply that clinicians are immigration attorneys.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use trauma-aware language, clear headings, readable type, meaningful alt text, WCAG AA contrast, visible focus states, and reduced-motion support. Forms should ask only for information needed to arrange a consultation and should warn users not to submit sensitive clinical or legal details through an unsecured contact form.
